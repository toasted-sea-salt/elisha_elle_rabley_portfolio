Consumer Brand · Houston, TX
The Anti-Cookie Co.

A small-batch food brand I founded: fudgy date-based bites with 9g of plant protein, no refined sugar, and no nuts, dairy, soy, or gluten. I built the brand and storefront end to end, including six flavors, a "Bite Flight" sampler box, build-your-own boxes, pre-orders, and a customer vote on new flavors.

What I learned: product development and pricing, building a brand from nothing, and designing a storefront around how customers actually buy.

Nonprofit · Small-Business Launch Lab
Cottage Business Lab

A pay-what-you-can nonprofit that helps hobbyists and first-time founders turn rough ideas into real small businesses. It offers help with naming and positioning, one-page business plans, website prototypes, a Virtual Farmers Market directory, free planning and pricing templates, and short founder guides.

What I learned: small-business consulting, designing services for people with no budget, and running intake through simple, low-cost workflows.

Resource Site · International Students
Elle's Guide

A plain-language immigration guide for McGill's international students, built from my own experience navigating the system. It includes step-by-step guides, a "Which Office?" tool that points students to the right campus or government office, student stories, curated resources, and site-wide search.

What I learned: information architecture, turning dense bureaucracy into clear UX writing, and researching and checking official sources.

AI Web App · Color Analysis & Styling
Atelier

An AI personal-stylist app. It runs a seasonal color analysis and body-shape profile, then builds a digital wardrobe from your photos. Claude identifies and tags each piece by category, formality, and pattern. An Outfit Studio, saved looks, and a wishlist turn the analysis into outfits you can actually wear.

What I learned: integrating an LLM into a real product, prompt design for image analysis, and building interfaces around AI output.

Data Storytelling · Live Dashboard
Humans Doing Things

"Right Now" is a live portrait of humanity this second: how many people are sleeping, laughing, or falling in love, plus heartbeats, CO₂ emitted, and emails sent. The counters tick in real time, using rates drawn from UN, WHO, and NASA data and written up as short editorial notes.

What I learned: turning annual statistics into per-second models, building a live-updating page with Claude Code, and editorial design for data.
