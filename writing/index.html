Policy Brief Public Health × Trade

Removing Red Dye No. 3 from Canada's Food Supply: A Policy Blueprint for the Minister of Health

CANS 402 · McGill University · Winter 2026

A strategy memo addressed to Canada's Minister of Health. It recommends removing Erythrosine (Red Dye No. 3), a colourant linked to cancer in animal studies, from Canada's permitted food colours by 2027 and from pharmaceuticals by 2028, ahead of the U.S. FDA's ban.

Applies Kingdon's Multiple Streams Framework to show a policy window opened by the July 2026 CUSMA renewal and U.S.–Canada trade tensions.
Maps a regulatory path that needs no Act of Parliament: a Governor in Council amendment through Canada Gazette I and II.
Builds a Cabinet coalition with the Trade and Finance ministers, and a health-equity framing for children and low-income communities.
Rebuts the "market will reformulate" objection by requiring natural substitutes over Red Dye 40.

Research Paper Cardiology · Health Equity

Mitigating a Global Health Crisis: Proposals Towards Improving Automated External Defibrillator Accessibility

Advanced Topics in Biological Research Writing · Stanford Online High School · 2022–2023

Sudden cardiac arrest kills about 9 in 10 people it strikes outside a hospital, and about 70% of those arrests happen at home, away from public AEDs. This paper reviews the cardiac physiology behind defibrillation and the racial and income gaps in survival. It then proposes practical ways to put AEDs within reach during the critical minutes.

Proposes "Medical Emergency Boxes," neighborhood AED stations modeled on Little Free Libraries, with placement rules for suburban and rural areas.
Argues for a built-in "Find My AED" phone feature, and for smartwatch software that alerts contacts or 911 when it detects cardiac arrest.
Informed by the Stanford Surgical Skills Summer Internship (2022) and consultation with Stanford cardiologists.

Policy Proposal Political Philosophy

Innovating the Electoral System for the Modern Era: A Proposal for Temporal Reform in the U.S. Presidential Election

PHIL 446 · Contemporary Issues in Political Philosophy · McGill University · Prof. Daniel Weinstock · April 2024

Proposes extending U.S. Election Day into a 72-hour, Sunday-to-Tuesday voting window to reduce turnout gaps driven by work schedules, commutes, and long lines. It pairs the extension with a Live Election Tracker (LET), a real-time public results platform.

Designs LET to build trust by removing the "black box" between counting and certification, and to fight voter fatigue with civic-education features.
Engages the strongest objections head-on: unequal access, information gaps between voting days, strategic voting, and the bandwagon effect.

Essay Political Philosophy × Media

Exploring Multidimensional Influencer Culture through a Marxian Lens

PHIL 445 · 19th-Century Political Philosophy · McGill University · April 2024

Reads modern influencer culture through Marx's theory of commodity fetishism in Capital. The essay argues that influencers commodify identity and lifestyle in two ways, Influencer Marketing and Influencer Branding, and in doing so obscure the social relationship with their audience.

Treats "likes" as a social currency, which explains why people with no paid following still post.
Looks at the consequences for young audiences and for influencers' own sense of identity.
